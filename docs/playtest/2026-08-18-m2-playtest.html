Playtest script · M2

Felkyo M2 Playtest

Does a creature feel like something you keep — or like a page you visit?

M2 gave every creature a mood that changes while you are away, treats to give it, a card that keeps it beside you, and a shop that sells creatures for gems. Daily adoption is gone. This script is how we find out whether any of that lands with somebody who was not in the room while it was built.

One session 35–45 min
Testers needed 1 + a watcher
Test on Phone first
Before you start

Setting up

  1. Use a phone for the whole first half. Most of what changed in M2 was decided on a 360px screen, and most of Skerro's notes were about phones. Move to a laptop only where the script says so.
  2. Have a second account signed in elsewhere — another browser, or the demo accounts. Earning gems means petting somebody else's creature, so a tester alone in the world cannot earn anything.
  3. Do not help. This is the whole method. Read the sentence in quotes, then stop talking. If the tester asks "where do I click?", the answer is "wherever you think" — and note the question, because it is a finding.
  4. Write down the moment they hesitate, not just whether they got there. A task completed after twenty confused seconds is a failed task.

Ticking a box here does not save anything — the page forgets when you close it. Keep the real notes on paper or in the findings sheet at the bottom.

Part one · watch, don't help

The first ten minutes

These are in the order a new player would live them. Read the quoted sentence aloud and nothing else. What we are testing is golden rule 1: if it needs explaining, it isn't designed yet.

01 · Arriving

Get yourself into Felkyo and find out what you have.

What to watch
  • Do they find their way in? A logged-out visitor now gets no sidebar — the only door is the log in pill in the top row. That is new, and it is the thing most likely to be wrong.
  • Once in: do they realise they have a creature, and do they go to it without being told?
  • On the creature's page, what do they read first? It should be the sentence — "Biscuit is very happy." If their eye goes to the numbers instead, the mood line is not doing its job.
02 · Caring

Your creature has been on its own for a while. Do something nice for it.

What to watch
  • Petting should be immediate. Do they see the happiness bar move? If they miss it, the animation is too quiet.
  • Do they discover the treats they already own? Every new player is given three. Nobody is told this in words — the chooser is simply there. If they never notice it, that is a real finding.
  • Do they read what a treat does before choosing, or pick the first one? The three differ in kind on purpose — if that difference is invisible, the choice is decoration.
  • Ask afterwards: "what would happen if you didn't come back for a week?" The honest answer is "it gets sleepy and is pleased to see you". If they say "it would be sad" or "it might die", the wording is frightening people and needs softening.
03 · Keeping

Keep this creature where you can see it, wherever you go on the site.

What to watch
  • Do they find the star? It is right under the creature. Before M2 this lived in the profile settings, which nobody opened.
  • After starring, do they notice the card appear? It is at the bottom of the strip on a phone. If they scroll past it, the placement is wrong.
  • Do they work out that they can pet and feed from the card, without opening the creature? That is the entire point of the feature.
  • Watch whether pressing the star twice confuses them. It is a toggle — the word changes from "make favourite" to "favourite".
Part two · watch, don't help

Earning and spending

This is the loop the whole site now turns on: gems come from visiting other people's creatures, and go on your own. If a tester cannot join those two halves up on their own, the loop does not exist for them.

04 · Wanting

You'd like a second creature. See if you can get one.

What to watch
  • Where do they look first? Shop is correct. If they hunt for an "adopt" button, the change from daily adoption has left a hole.
  • A new player cannot afford one. Read them the refusal: it should say how many gems short they are and where gems come from. Ask whether they now know what to do.
  • Note whether they try to press a button that looks unavailable. Nothing is greyed out on purpose — a disabled button says "no" and nothing else.
05 · Visiting

Go and find somebody else's creature, and say hello to it.

What to watch
  • Do they find community? Browsing creatures and finding people are two tabs on one page now — they used to be two separate pills.
  • When they pet a stranger's creature, do they notice they were paid? The message says so. If they miss it, the loop's one reward is invisible.
  • Then ask: "how would you get enough for that creature you wanted?" If the answer is "keep doing this", the loop has landed. If they shrug, it hasn't.
  • Does anything make them feel they are farming rather than visiting? That feeling is the failure mode of paying the clicker.

Part four

Two things that need a decision, not a test

These came out of building M2. No amount of watching a tester will settle them — they are the Product Owner's call.

The second creature may be too easy to afford

1 gem per pet · 30 s cooldown per creature · 12 creatures on the browse page · 50 gems for the cheapest creature · 100 gems a day, at most

Twelve creatures are reachable at a time and the cooldown is half a minute, so a determined player earns roughly 24 gems a minute. That puts a new creature about two minutes away, and the daily ceiling about four.

Our read: that makes the cap an anti-script measure rather than pacing, which is fine — but it also means a second creature costs almost nothing. Watch the tester's face when they buy one. If it does not feel earned, the lever to pull is the price, not the cooldown: a longer cooldown punishes the honest player and barely inconveniences anyone else.

Two art questions, both optional

A sleepy sprite. A dozing creature currently reads as dozing through its words, its energy bar, and a slightly softened picture. A real drawing would be lovelier; it is one more image per species.

Favourite treats. One column and one line of copy would let a creature prefer something — the difference between feeding a stat and feeding a character. Cheap to add, and nothing depends on it.
