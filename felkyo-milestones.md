# Felkyo Creatures — Milestone Plan

Updated after Skerro's answers. Two parts: what has to exist **before we open registration**, and what we grow **while it's live**. Every milestone is a proper finishing point.

Changes from the last version are marked ⭐.

---

## PART ONE — Before we open the doors

### M1 — Identity, items, and the words people choose
- Player profiles with **avatars from a set we provide** (never uploaded)
- Profile personalisation from menus: avatar, colour scheme, featured creatures, owned frames and trinkets
- Coloured item cards (colour + icon + label, so category reads at a glance)
- Item detail view, sell and discard
- The underlying decision on how items, creatures, plants and fish are stored
- **Player search and public profiles** — search by name, no "newest members" list, opt-out available
- Name and bio safety: length limits, bad-word filter, anti-impersonation, **no links or contact details**
- Report button on every visible name, bio and profile
- ⭐ **Report categories** so serious ones can jump the queue
- ⭐ **Reported bios and cards auto-hide** as "under review" until someone looks — protects players in the hours before a human is awake
- ⭐ **Report acknowledgement**: "We've received this and will look into it"

### M2 — The creator's panel ⭐ heavily expanded
- ⭐ **Roles: owner, moderator, artist, coder** — each sees only what they need. Someone can moderate without getting the keys to everything.
- ⭐ **One page per creature.** Every stage's sprite, stats, per-stage blurbs, library art and lore, all on a single screen. Upload it all in one go, publish when it's ready. *This is aimed squarely at the thing that burned you out last time.*
- Image upload for sprites, icons, portraits, card art, backgrounds
- ⭐ **One-click art replacement** — drop in the new file, everything keeps pointing at it, no cached old version
- ⭐ **Preview before publishing** — see the creature on its real page, at any stage, on phone and desktop
- ⭐ **"What's unfinished" list** — the site tells you what's missing (a species with no adult sprite, an item with no icon)
- ⭐ **Bulk table entry for loot** — type or paste straight into a live table, with the forage/hunt/mine/fish category layout duplicable between areas
- ⭐ **Undo / revert to previous version** on content screens
- ⭐ **Moderation queue with account history** — one page showing everything an account has done, so patterns are visible instead of judging each report alone
- ⭐ **Official notices to players** — a card telling them what happened, with the Felkyo email for appeals. One-way; players can't reply
- ⭐ **Stats page** — what's being adopted, bought, visited. Replaces the Google doc
- Editable settings: cooldowns, XP thresholds, daily limits, registration on/off, the word list
- Named maintenance buttons for specific jobs

**Done when:** Skerro adds a complete new creature — every stage, stats, lore, library entry — alone, start to finish.

### M3 — The pound, and who a creature is
- Send a creature to the pound; three-day grace period with reclaim
- Adopt a creature someone else rehomed
- Original owner can't re-adopt after the grace period
- Forbidding outside, cosy inside
- ⭐ **Personality traits in two tiers** — ones a creature *earns* by living, and ones the **world gives it**: a shelter trait or two on leaving the pound, and "grateful" as a lucky extra on adoption. Traits are data you edit in the panel.
- ⭐ **Traits are flavour, plus one small XP bonus. Nothing else.** They never change a stat or a price. *(Combat effects wait for the arena in M13 — deciding them now would mean designing a fighting system by accident, one trait at a time.)*
- ⭐ **Freeze a creature at a stage you love.** Growth pauses. Unfreeze and it works through the stages it missed, one at a time, so you still get to watch it grow up. Nothing is lost and a frozen creature still earns you coins.
- ⭐ **Level titles and a friendly age** — "level 7" also reads as a word, and "born 3 August" also reads as "a few weeks old". Both are lists you can edit.

### M4 — Themes and accessibility
- Selectable colour themes, addable through the panel
- ⭐ **Contrast checked automatically while you pick colours** — the panel warns you before a theme ships unreadable
- ⭐ **A dyslexia-friendly mode** (font and spacing), offered as its own theme

### M5 — The library ⭐ new milestone
- A page per species: every stage, its blurb, and the longer lore
- ⭐ **A short blurb per stage, plus a full story in the library book** — both, as you suggested
- Book graphics you click to open, like the old wiki
- ⭐ **Alternate forms** for species, shown here too
- ⭐ **Newspaper articles about the NPCs**
- All of it edited from the same one-page-per-creature screen in M2

*Moved early because it's the pokédex — it's how people work out what's out there and what to hope for.*

### M6 — Postcards, bottles, and company
- Friends list as a **bookmark** — quick access to pet and send cards. Unlocks nothing.
- Cards bought at the post office, **sendable to anyone**, displayed publicly on the recipient's profile
- **Message in a bottle** — pre-written, set adrift, found at random. Nobody picks who finds it.
- ⭐ **Clicking someone else's creature now rewards both sides** — they get the growth, you get gems. Logged-out visitors get nothing.
- ⭐ **A copy-paste share box** on every creature page — ready-made HTML with the image and link, for posting on forums and other sites
- Reporting and blocking across cards, bottles and guestbook

### M7 — Opening up
- Full security review
- Data protection: privacy policy, real account deletion, GDPR
- Written position on young players and how reports get handled
- ⭐ **A gentle first-run introduction** — Ruinily's chat bubbles, plus a permanent "don't show me this again" button. No forced tutorial.
- ⭐ **A feedback route** (the Felkyo email) alongside reports
- Registration opens to a small group first, then widens

## PART TWO — Growing the world (live)

### M8 — Care
- Feeding, cleaning, sickness and cure
- ⭐ **A well-cared-for creature earns double gems when clicked** — the one care bonus, kept deliberately simple
- ⭐ **Badges and trophies** for long-kept creatures (100 days and up)
- Neglect never kills or permanently harms a creature

### M9 — Shops and areas become plural
- Multi-shop engine with NPC portraits and dialogue; Ruinily gets her real lines
- ⭐ **Seasonal stock** — every shop listing can carry a "from" and "to" date, so Valentine chocolates in February or the Gallows at Halloween is a date you type, not a job for a developer
- Multi-area world with an area index
- Both manageable from the panel

### M10 — The world opens up
- Twelve areas; foraging, fishing, mining, hunting
- Tool progression; weather per area
- All loot tables entered through the panel
- Bottles start turning up while fishing and gathering
- ⭐ **The fish tank, right after fishing** — decorate it with what you own, arrange by tapping or dragging

### M11 — Crafting and your own hub
- Recipe engine; cooking with Yumie; potions with Luca
- ⭐ **Your house as a hub you build up** — once you've earned the stations, you work from home instead of borrowing the shops'
- ⭐ **Decorating your room and page uses things you own** — bought, kept in your inventory, placed from a menu. Not the old free-form sticker-and-font layer, which is its own year-long project and lets a page end up broken on a phone. Placement will always work by tapping and by keyboard, never dragging only. *(The screens get designed when this milestone starts.)*

### M12 — Farming
- Seed to harvest, with the free daily click so rare visitors still grow things
- Fertiliser as a shortcut, never a requirement

### M13 — Long tail
- **Quests with Yuni** — kept at the end, as you wanted: the world will be built up and the guild quests released by then
- Beachcombing and the remaining themed shops

## Deliberately not building
- **No trading** for now — revisited only if the community actually asks
- No gifting arbitrary items, no player shops or auctions, no cross-player breeding
- The line: players send each other warmth **through words we wrote** — not value, not words of their own

## Three things you asked

**Card size, and is the text part of the art?**
Recommendation: the art is the background, the site puts the words on top. That way one card design can carry different messages, the text stays sharp at any size, it's editable without redrawing, and screen readers can read it. So: draw them with a calm area where text can sit, and don't letter them by hand. Size — make them comfortably larger than they'll display so they stay crisp on big screens.

**Per-stage blurbs and library books?**
Yes, both, exactly as you described. A short blurb on each stage where people meet it, and the long version in the library book. Both edited on the same screen as the sprites.

**How hard is it to change things later — does it get rebuilt?**
No. It's built in small pieces that are swapped or added individually, which is why we go milestone by milestone. **You do not need to get the first version perfect.** Changing a value is a text edit. Changing a look is a theme edit. Adding a creature is you, alone, in the panel. Even reworking a whole feature touches only that feature. Lock in what you're sure about, leave the rest open.

## On designing pages without touching code

You'll get, through the panel:
- **Layouts chosen from a set**, with live preview
- **Drag to reorder** the sections on creature and profile pages — with up/down buttons too, so it also works by keyboard
- **A visual theme editor**, colours picked and previewed, contrast checked as you go
- **Every piece of art swappable in one click**

What you won't get is a blank canvas where anything goes anywhere. A freeform page builder is its own year-long project, and it would let a page be built that breaks on phones or fails contrast. Templates plus reordering plus themes gets you nearly all of the control with none of that risk.

**On a console:** the panel gets named buttons for specific jobs instead. A web page that runs arbitrary commands is the most dangerous thing that can exist on a public site — whoever reaches it owns the server and every player's account. The things a console would have been *for* — seeing what's happening, checking what's unfinished, previewing changes — are all covered above.
